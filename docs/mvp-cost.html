What "MVP" means here

Scope discipline is the single biggest lever on the number, so it is stated first.

The MVP is the smallest system that can sit in a real home, with a real family and a real caregiver, safely and lawfully. That definition closes every blocking gap in the current POC and deliberately defers everything else.

A Raspberry Pi 5 with a Hailo-8L accelerator comfortably runs MoveNet Lightning across three streams at 8 fps, which is what the detector needs. A Jetson Orin Nano would roughly double the device cost and buys headroom the MVP does not use — worth revisiting only when multi-person tracking is in scope.

Two things stand out. First, engineering maintenance dominates at pilot scale — two retained engineers spread across 200 homes is ₹2,500 per home per month, nine times everything else combined. A pilot is not a business; it is a fixed cost divided by a small number.

Second, alert delivery is negligible at roughly ₹2 per home per month. Push is free, SMS is a fallback, and voice escalation is rare. The instinct to worry about messaging cost is misplaced here.

Why edge inference is the cost decision

The same product with cloud-side inference costs 18–42× more to run at scale.

This is the most consequential architectural decision in the system, and it was made for privacy reasons — raw video never leaves the home. The cost advantage is a by-product. It is worth saying in that order, because it means the privacy position does not have to be defended on cost grounds, and the cost position does not have to be defended on privacy grounds.

Assumptions and what would break them

Stated so the model can be argued with rather than trusted.

  • Connectivity is free. The model assumes the household already has reliable broadband. The Health Affairs summit named rural broadband as an unresolved obstacle; where it fails, the edge device needs local buffering and an LTE fallback — add roughly ₹350–600 per home per month.
  • Salaries are India-metro 2026. A tier-2 city team runs 25–35% lower. Hiring a CV specialist may take longer than the build allows, which is a schedule risk before it is a cost risk.
  • Three camera streams per home. More rooms means a larger edge device, or a second one.
  • 36-month hardware life. Consumer-grade single-board computers in Indian summer heat may not reach it. A 24-month assumption raises the amortised figure by half.
  • No clinical validation study. The MVP includes calibration, not evidence of outcomes. A proper validation trial is ₹25–50L and belongs to the next phase — but it is what any payer or hospital partner will eventually ask for.
  • No field-service network. Costed as install labour only. At scale, device failure and re-siting need a service model this model does not contain.
  • Customer acquisition excluded entirely. Sales, marketing and channel are out of scope here. In this category they are frequently larger than the technology cost.
The honest headline

Building it is not the hard part. ₹68L–₹1.7 Cr is a modest engineering programme, and unit economics at ₹74–178 per home per month are comfortable against any plausible subscription price. The binding constraints are the ones all three sources converge on — whether families adopt it, whether caregivers keep responding to it, and whether it can be shown to change an outcome. None of those are solved by spending more on the build.
